Where Curiosity Met Creativity

Growing up, I was fascinated by cartoons, especially Dexter’s Laboratory. Watching Dexter build all sorts of inventions sparked something in me. I wanted to create things of my own, even before I fully understood how they worked.

I started dismantling broken toys, scavenging for useful parts like motors, switches, and lights, and using them to build my own little creations. Not everything worked as planned, but I enjoyed experimenting, figuring things out, and discovering what I could make from what I had.

I also grew up in a home where computers and technology were part of the environment. You could say we were a family of computer geeks. Growing up in that environment made computers and technology a familiar part of my world, giving my curiosity even more room to grow.

While technology was a shared interest in our family, I gradually found my own way of expressing it. At home, I became the unofficial handyman, the person ready to investigate almost anything that needed fixing. From electronics to broken door handles, blocked sinks, and faulty taps, I was always willing to give it a try. Somehow, no matter what was broken, I was usually the one expected to look into it!

Looking back, these were just ordinary parts of growing up, but they give a glimpse of where some of my interests began. I didn’t have a clear career path in mind back then. I was simply curious about how things worked, interested in trying things for myself, and figuring things out as I went along.

When the Plan Changed

My first step into university life was at Abubakar Tafawa Balewa University (ATBU), Bauchi, where I went to study Electrical Engineering.

Circumstances made attending in person difficult, and I sometimes struggled to keep up with lectures.

Eventually, I had to take a step back and consider my options. I began exploring other ways to continue my education, and that search led me to the National Open University of Nigeria (NOUN).

It wasn’t the route I had originally planned, but it offered another way forward. Looking back, that experience taught me that sometimes moving forward means being willing to reconsider your plans and find a path that better fits your circumstances.

My journey took a different direction, but my interest in technology remained. I continued my education through Computer Science, opening the door to explore areas that had interested me for years while discovering new ones along the way.

A Different Route, New Possibilities

Joining the National Open University of Nigeria changed more than where I studied. The flexibility it offered gave me room to explore other opportunities alongside my degree, including the 3MTT programme and TVET training in Electrical and Solar Installation.

Sometimes, I look back and wonder if things happened the way they were meant to. I had initially set out to study Electrical Engineering at ATBU, but circumstances led me down a different path. Yet, somehow, I found myself learning about electrical systems again, this time through hands-on training.

Through TVET, I’m getting the opportunity to work with electrical installations and solar systems, connecting what I learn with practical experience. For me, there’s something valuable about being able to handle the tools, work with actual components, and see how things come together beyond what can be learned from theory alone.

In a way, it feels like a part of my original plan found its way back into my life, just through a different door.

At the same time, 3MTT opened another avenue for me to explore software development and build my skills in technology. Rather than following just one path, I’ve been able to learn across different fields, discover new interests, and find ways to connect them.

I don’t know if I would have had these same experiences if everything had gone according to my original plan. But I’m learning that a change of direction doesn’t always mean losing sight of where you wanted to go. Sometimes, it leads you to opportunities you might never have considered otherwise.

Finding My Way into Software Development

Before I joined 3MTT, I had already started exploring programming on my own. I tried learning Python through YouTube tutorials, hoping to build on my interest in computers and understand how software was made. However, self-learning didn’t quite work out the way I had hoped, and I struggled to make the progress I wanted.

Rather than letting that be the end of my attempt, I kept looking for another opportunity to learn. That’s how I discovered the 3MTT programme and applied for the Software Development track.

The programme introduced me to HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, giving me a starting point in web development. It was a new direction from my initial attempt with Python, and it gave me an opportunity to approach programming through building for the web.

Completing the programme and receiving my certificate was an important milestone for me. It represented more than just finishing a training programme; it was a step forward from my earlier attempts to learn programming independently.

My journey in software development is still ongoing, but 3MTT helped me move from watching tutorials and trying to figure things out alone towards learning through a more structured programme and putting those skills into practice.

It was one of the first steps in a journey that continues to grow as I explore new tools, work on projects, and discover what I can build with what I’ve learned.

From Building Software to Securing It

My journey into technology began taking shape through software development. Through 3MTT, I was introduced to web technologies like HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, and I began exploring how applications are built. But as my interest in technology grew, I became curious about another side of it: security.

Building software is one thing; understanding how to keep systems and the information they handle secure is another. That curiosity led me towards cybersecurity, a field I’m now exploring through the Digital Training Academy (DTA) programme and Coursera-based learning.

It’s a shift in focus, but not a complete departure from where I started. Software development introduced me to the process of building digital solutions, while cybersecurity is opening my eyes to the importance of protecting them.

I’m still learning and discovering which areas of cybersecurity interest me most. For now, this is another step in my journey, expanding my understanding of technology beyond creating applications to thinking about their security, too.
